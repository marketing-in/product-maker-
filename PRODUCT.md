# Product

**Teardown Lab** turns a description or photos of a physical product into a part-by-part teardown: an exploded 3D model, the wiring between parts with voltage, current and power, a bill of materials, and Blender / STEP exports.

## Who uses it

- Repair technicians and refurbishers working out what is inside before opening a unit.
- Sourcing and cost engineers estimating a bill of materials from a competitor or a sample.
- Automation and electronics engineers, and students, learning how a product class is built.

They sit at a desk or a bench in daylight, usually with the product next to the keyboard and often with a meter in hand. They look up from the product, find a part on screen, read what it is and what to measure, and look back down.

## Mode

Operate. This is a tool. The model and the data are the subject; the interface recedes. Scanability, stable layout and honest labelling outrank expression.

## Voice

Plain workshop English. Short declarative sentences. Name the part, say what it does, say how sure we are. Never dress an estimate as a measurement.

## Principles

1. **Evidence before confidence.** Every part and connection is marked observed, inferred or assumed, coded by shape (full, half, empty disc), with the evidence and the open question next to it.
2. **One model, many views.** The 3D view, power tree, BOM and exports all read the same teardown, so selecting a part in one selects it everywhere.
3. **Numbers are data.** Voltages, currents, sizes and reference designators are set in the monospace face with tabular figures.
4. **Colour means something.** Wire colours encode the kind of connection. Amber means "selected" and nothing else.
